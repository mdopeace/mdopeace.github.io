How I think

  1. 01 / Notice

    It starts with films. And with the search for one worth sitting through. Streaming splits across separate subscriptions, and whatever is free is buried in ads and buffering — so I download, and watch it dark, on a laptop.

  2. 02 / Make

    Which made the player the bottleneck. None of them felt like they belonged on a Mac. So I built one that did — native, keyboard driven, mine.

    And then it started over. The first version shipped, and I wasn't satisfied with it. The fixes kept arriving and evaporating mid-task — the next idea, gone before I could write it down. So I built somewhere to put them.

    With the list finally captured, I can go back and finish what I started.

QuickTodo

  • macOS 13+
  • Intel or Apple Silicon

Why I built it

A todo app that lives in your Mac menu bar — for the things that don't survive the meeting.

The thinking behind it

Native SwiftUI. Add with Return, open with ⌥⇧Space. No window to manage, no account, no Electron. Standup hands you five things. You go deep on one, and the other four evaporate before you can touch them. So they land here instead — where they can't be lost, which leaves your attention on the work in front of you, and shows you what you actually finished by the end of the day. Day-grouped, repeats daily or weekly, rename in place, drag to reorder. It also keeps itself current: verifies the download's SHA-256, then replaces the running bundle in place. 65KB of tests, including the installer and the relaunch after it.

Vidp

  • macOS 26+
  • Apple Silicon

Why I built it

A keyboard-driven video player built on libmpv — for the film you already downloaded.

The thinking behind it

You have the file. You double-click it. Then the player you've got either looks like it belongs to 2009, or wants four clicks past a licence agreement before it shows you anything. So: AppKit, mpv, OpenGL. Drag a file in, or hit it from Finder. mp4, m4v, mov, mkv, webm, avi. It ships its own libmpv and FFmpeg, hands off to the macOS now-playing controls, updates itself from the GitHub API, and uninstalls without leaving anything behind. Releases are arm64, so an Intel machine has to build it from source. I use it every day. Playback, everyday controls, and deeper customization are already there. Now I'm exploring the extras I want for myself, including ways to give an offline collection the feel of a streaming library.

Cookieater

  • Browser Extension
  • Manifest V3

Why I built it

A friendly Chrome and Firefox extension for viewing, searching, and editing cookies on the current site.

The thinking behind it

See the active site's cookies at a glance, search names, values, and domains, then edit individual cookie settings or add and remove cookies. Import cookies from a JSON array, export the current domain's cookies, or clear them all after confirmation. Cookieater handles cookie data locally in your browser; it doesn't send your data to a Cookieater server.
